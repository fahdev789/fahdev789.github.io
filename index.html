Research log —

I study where reasoning behaviour and final answers come apart.

I'm an independent AI safety researcher and machine-learning engineer focused on reasoning models, evaluations, chain-of-thought controllability, capability elicitation, and the gap between what a model appears to reason about and what its final answer actually complies with.

What I'm actually working on

active

Reasoning & compliance

"A strong reasoning trace does not guarantee a compliant final answer."

Testing whether reasoning models can satisfy constraints internally and in their final outputs, and measuring when those behaviours decouple.

active

Evaluations & red-teaming

"The useful eval is the one that exposes a failure we did not already expect."

Building controlled evaluations, adversarial tests, and capability-elicitation experiments that probe beyond benchmark scores.

active

Interpretability & monitoring

"Behavioural evidence becomes stronger when we can inspect what changes underneath it."

Exploring attention, entropy, representations, and lightweight monitoring signals for detecting changes in model behaviour.

Current pre-deployment evals catch most dangerous capabilities

Evals are good at what they're built to test for, and blind to what they weren't. That asymmetry worries me.

Reasoning-model evaluations will increasingly need to measure process and outcome separately
